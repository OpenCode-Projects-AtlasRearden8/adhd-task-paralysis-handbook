The Stuck-Task Ladder

For when you're too stuck to read chapters. Read one rung. Do it, even if it feels beneath you — that feeling is the paralysis talking. If it doesn't move anything, go up one rung. Never skip.

  1. Rung 1Change your body's inputs. Water. A cracker. Light on your face. Stand up, sit somewhere else. Paralysis is sometimes just a body complaining. No task action yet.
  2. Rung 2Write the task as one sentence. The next thing, not the project: "email the landlord about the pipe."
  3. Rung 3Shrink it in half. Then half again. "Open email, type the address in the To line." Yes, that tiny. Tiny is the point.
  4. Rung 4Open the door. Stand there. Do rung 3's action. Don't continue. That's a completed rep — mark it. If you want more: five-minute timer, permission to stop at the buzzer.
  5. Rung 5Recruit a body double. Text a friend: no advice, no help — just be near me for 30 minutes while I do my thing. No human? A study-with-me video counts.
  6. Rung 6Ask the AI shrinker. "I'm stuck on [task]. 5 possible first steps, each under 2 minutes and physical. No plans, no advice." Then say: "even smaller."
  7. Rung 7Timer plus witness. "I'm doing five minutes while you sit here." Say the deal out loud. Stop at the buzzer or keep going — either counts.
  8. Rung 8Dump it on paper. Circle one. Everything on the task, all the worry included. Circle one item. Cross out the rest for today — not deleted, archived: "not today."
  9. Rung 9Lower the stakes on purpose. "This doesn't have to be good. It has to be started." Draft the ugly version. The rough one gets replies; the perfect one never leaves your head.
  10. Rung 10Veto or renegotiate. Does it need doing? Now? This way? Say the yes out loud — chosen tasks start easier than assigned ones.

Wherever you got: mark it, even one mark. Zero days add nothing and subtract nothing — what's already on the card stays.
