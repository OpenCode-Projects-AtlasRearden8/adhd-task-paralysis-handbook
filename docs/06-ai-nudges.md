# Chapter 6 — AI Nudges That Actually Help

You already have access to a patient, never-tired, never-judging brainstorm
partner for the exact job paralysis defeats: **finding the first step.** Used
wrong, AI becomes another procrastination appliance. Used right, it's an
on-ramp. The difference is one rule.

## The one rule

> **Use AI to find and shrink the next step — never to do the step for you,
> and never to plan the whole thing.**

"Plan my taxes" produces a beautiful document and zero doors opened. That's a
.new, fancier place to be stuck. "What's a two-second first move on my taxes?"
produces an actual move.

## The three nudges that work

### Nudge 1: The shrinker (the main event)

When a task is a fog-blob, tell your AI exactly that. Real, tested prompt:

> "I'm stuck on: [task]. I have ADHD-style task paralysis, so don't give me a
> plan, don't give me advice, just give me 5 possible FIRST STEPS, each one
> two minutes or less and physical. Numbers only."

Then two magic words: **"even smaller."** Whatever it gives you, ask again —
"make #2 even smaller." You are not looking for a good plan. You are
prospecting for a **doorway**.

### Nudge 2: The declumper

Sometimes the fog is a knot of eight intertwined tasks pretending to be one:

> "Here's everything spinning around in my head about [task/week]: [dump it
> all, unsorted]. Sort it for me into: (1) things that DON'T actually need
> doing, (2) things someone else must decide, (3) five-minute actions, (4)
> one real next action for today. Short answers only."

The "things that DON'T need doing" bucket routinely contains a third of the
pile. Deleting ghosts is getting stuck unstuck.

### Nudge 3: The hype-lamp (for the Dread Flavored tasks)

For tasks that are boring *and* stinging — the dispute letter, the benefits
form, the awkward email:

> "I have to [task] and I keep avoiding it because [it feels huge / I don't
> know the etiquette / I'm scared of getting it wrong]. Don't do it for me.
> Say one true encouraging sentence, then give me the smallest possible first
> physical action. Then remind me I can stop after that."

It works because flattery-adjacent warmth from a machine costs it nothing to
replenish — you can ask again tomorrow, and the week after, and it will never
sigh, never say "we've talked about this."

## Anti-patterns: where AI nudges go wrong

- **Research spiral.** "AI helped me plan the garage" becomes 40 minutes of
  comparing shelving units you haven't bought. If you're asking follow-up
  questions that aren't "even smaller?", you've left the doorway.
- **Advice-collecting.** Five frameworks for task management is reading, not
  starting. The shrinker prompt says "don't give me advice" for a reason.
- **Ghost task-creation.** Any "you could also…" answer gets logged as
  *optional* or ignored. It doesn't get to add weight.
- **The shame-bots.** Any assistant tone that starts moralizing ("why don't
  you just…") or performance-managing streaks: drop it. This manual's tools
  work because they're kind; an AI that can't stay kind is a broken tool, not
  a you problem.

## Privacy notes (plain English)

- Chatting with a major AI chatbot about "I can't start my taxes" is similar
  to emailing that sentence to a company: fine for most purposes. **Don't
  paste account numbers, SSNs, medical records, downloadable client files, or
  other identifiers.** Describe tasks generically: "a benefits form," not a
  copy of the form with your data filled in.
- Local and privacy-first options exist (tools like ChatGPT's temporary
  chats, Claude, Gemini, Ollama running models on your own machine). If that
  matters to you, a local model works fine for the shrinker prompt — it's a
  small task.
- This book takes no stance on which AI you use. If yours is slow or preachy,
  another one makes the same tool work better.

## One minute of setup, then done

Paste these two lines somewhere you'll find them (a phone note, a stickies
file). You'll never need to remember the prompts:

```
Shrinker:  "I'm stuck on [task]. 5 possible FIRST STEPS, each under 2
minutes and physical. No plans, no advice. Then I'll say 'even smaller.'"

Declumper: "Sort this dump into: not-actually-needed / not-mine / 5-minute
actions / one real next action today: [everything]"
```
