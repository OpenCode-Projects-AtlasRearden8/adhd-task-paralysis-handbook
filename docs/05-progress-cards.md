# Chapter 5 — Visible Progress Cards

The paralysis loop has a cruel second half: even when you *do* work, invisible
work vanishes, and a brain that can't see progress concludes nothing happened.
So it won't start tomorrow either. This chapter makes effort physical.

## The idea

A **progress card** is a piece of paper (or a whiteboard, or sticky notes on
the wall) where each small act of starting gets marked, in ink, where you can
see it. Not a digital to-do list — those work for days you're already moving.
This is for the days when your brain needs *evidence*.

## The paper tallies (the cheap classic)

One sheet of paper per task. One tally mark each time you do a doorway or a
five-minute session. Two kinds of marks:

- **| ** a doorway (opened the door, even if nothing else happened)
- **X** a five-minute session (focused time in the room)

Five marks in a row, draw the cross line: **first rung.** Keep the sheet
*taped somewhere visible* — door frame, fridge, bathroom mirror. Three
principles do the work:

1. **Visible all the time.** Evidence in a drawer doesn't exist. Evidence on
   the doorframe gets seen on every walk to the kitchen — including by a
   brain currently insisting it never starts anything.
2. **One act, one mark.** No judgment of quality, size, or outcome. The mark
   says one thing only: *"the door opened."*
3. **The card survives bad days.** A zero day adds nothing and subtracts
   nothing. The marks that are already there don't get erased. That matters
   enormously — most self-tracking fails because a bad day feels like a
   verdict. Here it's just an empty spot.

## The task card (for long projects)

For tasks spanning weeks — taxes, a real project, the move — use one card per
task with **milestone sticky notes** on the wall:

```
[ FIND RECEIPTS ]   ← this sticky comes DOWN when done,
[ LIST DEDUCTIONS ] ← the pile of fallen stickies IS the progress
[ PRINT FORMS ]
[ MAIL PACKET ]
```

Moving stickies from "wall, to do" to "wall, did" is weirdly powerful for a
brain that needs progress to be *spatial and physical*. And moving a sticky
down a wall costs about one second of executive function.

## What NOT to do

- **No apps.** For now. An app adds setup friction and hides the evidence
  behind a screen you'd have to think to open. Paper first; migrate later if
  you outgrow it.
- **No streaks.** No flames or counts or "day 14 in a row." Streak-shame is
  just paralysis with a scoreboard.
- **No "must complete the row" rules.** Rows break. That's life. The card
  doesn't judge.

## The retirement ceremony

When a task is done, do something, off-the-books and silly: the sheet gets a
big **DONE** and goes up on the wall for one more day before it's tossed or
archived in a folder. A visible history of finished tasks is the opposite of
the shame narrative. It says: *"this brain opens doors, about so often,
empirically, month over month."*
