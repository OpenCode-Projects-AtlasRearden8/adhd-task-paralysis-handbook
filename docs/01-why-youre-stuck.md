# Chapter 1 — Why You're Stuck (this is not a character flaw)

Before any tricks, one explanation. Not to fix you — to stop you from
drafting the apology you don't owe anyone.

## The wall is real

Task paralysis (sometimes called *task initiation failure*) is what happens
when your brain can't convert "I should do that" into "doing it now." The
task isn't blocked by laziness. It's blocked at the **starting gate**, where
a tangled pile of things has to happen at once:

- **Deciding** — which task? which part? where to begin?
- **Sequencing** — what comes first, second, third?
- **Facing** — the dread, boredom, or ambiguity of the thing itself
- **Switching** — powering down whatever you were doing and powering up this

For brains that handle executive function easily, this all happens silently,
below notice. For ADHD and ADHD-adjacent brains, each step is a bill that
arrives separately — and the total is due *before the task even starts*.
So the brain does what it does with unaffordable bills: it looks away.

Then comes the shame tax. Not starting → feeling bad → dread grows → the task
gets more expensive to start → not starting. It's a loop, and the loop runs on
shame. **Remove the shame and the loop loses its fuel.** That's why this
manual is gentle on purpose. Gentleness isn't a tone here; it's the mechanism.

## The two useful facts

**1. Starting is a separate job from doing.**
The hard part is the ignition, not the drive. Every trick in this book works
because it makes ignition cheaper. Nobody has to want to do the dishes. They
only have to want to (or be able to) do the first eight seconds of the dishes.

**2. The task in your head is almost always bigger than the task in the room.**
In your head, "do taxes" is a sixty-hour monster involving receipts you can't
find, software you don't trust, and phone calls you dread. In the room, the
first actual bolt of work is "find the folder with W-2s" — ninety seconds.
Paralysis feeds on the fog. Every tool below is a fog-cutting tool.

## The shame audit (60 seconds)

When stuck, ask: **"What am I blaming myself for right now?"** Write the
answer down. Look at it. Most of the time it's something like "I should just
do it like a normal person." That sentence is doing you no work — it's pure
fuel for the loop. Cross it out. Pick any rung of the ladder instead.
