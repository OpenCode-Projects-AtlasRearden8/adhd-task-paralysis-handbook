# Chapter 2 — The Five-Minute Doorway Trick

The workhorse. If you learn one thing from this manual, learn this one.

## The trick

A task you can't start is a room you can't enter. So don't commit to the
room. Commit to the **doorway**.

1. **Name the task out loud or on paper.** Plain words: "the dishes," "the
   email to Marcus," "start the taxes folder."
2. **Shrink it to the doorway** — the smallest physical action that counts as
   *touching* the task. Not doing it. Touching it.
3. **Do only the doorway.** Then notice: you are now *inside the room*, and
   leaving has a cost too.

That's the whole physics: **starting friction is charged once; after the
doorway, momentum is slightly on your side.** Not guaranteed. Just reliably,
honestly tilted.

## The doorway menu

Doorways cost roughly **two to ninety seconds**. They should feel almost
embarrassingly small. Pick from the list or invent your own:

| The task | A doorway |
|---|---|
| Dishes | Put one fork in the sink. Or: run the water for five seconds. |
| Email | Open the email, type the recipient's name only. Done, door's open. |
| Taxes | Put the shoebox of receipts on the desk. That's it. |
| Report | Open the doc, title it, type one bullet of what it should say. |
| Phone call | Write the number on a sticky note. You don't even have to call today. |
| Laundry | Put the basket in the hallway. Progress. |
| Exercise | Put shoes on. Standing in shoes counts. |
| Reading | Open book to page one. Don't read. Permission not to. |
| Code / AI project | Open the file. Type one comment line describing next step. |

## The five minutes (the part people skip)

The word "five" earns its place. After the doorway, run a timer for **five
minutes** with this deal:

> "I will do *something* on this task for five minutes. If I want to stop at
> the timer, I stop with zero guilt. Stopping on time is a success, not a
> failure."

Two things happen. About half the time, five minutes of motion produces
enough momentum that you keep going — because **momentum lives in the task,
not in you.** The other half of the time, you stop at five minutes like a
professional. Either outcome is a win: one got the task moving, the other
taught your brain that starting doesn't trap you. That lesson directly lowers
starting friction every time you come back.

## Fine print (read once, then ignore forever)

- **You may do the doorway without the timer.** Some days opening the door is
  the whole victory. Log it, take the point, be done.
- **You may not use the timer to punish yourself.** "I only got five minutes
  done" is a sentence we do not say in this house.
- **Doorways can be resumed.** If you drift off mid-doorway, the next session
  starts from wherever you drifted. No restarting, no debt.

## If even the doorway won't budge

That's not the trick failing — it's a signal you need a *smaller* doorway or a
*different* tool. Halve the doorway until it's laughable: "open the notes
app." If even that's a no, this is a body-doubling day or a rung-one day on
the [Stuck-Task Ladder](07-stuck-task-ladder.md). Different wall, different
 equipment.
