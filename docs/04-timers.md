# Chapter 4 — Timers That Help (and One Kind That Hurts)

Time blindness means "I'll do it in a bit" never resolves, and "this will take
forever" always wins. Timers change the math — but only some timers.

## The three timers that help

### 1. The promise timer (the five minutes from Chapter 2)

Timer for **5 minutes**, promise: stopping at zero is a *success*. This is
with [the doorway trick](02-five-minute-doorway.md), never alone —
the timer box is worthless without a doorway leading into it.

### 2. The defiance timer

For pure *fog* days—when you don't even know where to start. Set a timer for
**10 minutes** and kill it writing **everything** you might do on the task:
every step, worry, half-thought. Unsorted, badly spelled, no paragraphs. When
the timer ends, throw the list away if it's junk. About half the time, it
isn't, and the list has doorways flat on its face. Defiance works because a
bad list can be thrown away, where a "real plan" is too heavy to lift.

### 3. The switch timer (the one you never expect to need)

Timers aren't only for starting. ADHD brains hyperfocus *too*: the dark
inverse of paralysis is a 4-hour detour on one corner of the task, or 45
minutes of "quick email checks." When you start a session, set a timer
**simultaneously** for when you'll check in with reality:

- Started a task with an outside deadline? Timer at 25 or 45 minutes.
- Opened "one tab" or "one email"? Timer at **10 minutes**, hard.

If the timer goes off and you're in flow on something truly useful, by all
means, keep going—but decide that *deliberately*, at the boundary, not at noon
on day three.

## The timer that hurts

The **big chunk timer**: "I'll do it for two hours." For a stuck brain, a
two-hour commitment is not a container; it is boulders. It has no lie-detecting
power when you get derailed at seven minutes in, and so it becomes a weapon
for self-shaming. Long chunks only ever come later, once a run of 5-minute
wins has proven the room is survivable, and they will be *chosen*, not
defaulted to.

## Practical setup

- Any timer works: phone, kitchen, web, a smart speaker, anything that gets
  noticed and doesn't take 4 menus to reach.
- Maker's rule: **no timer on your phone** if your phone is the thing that
  eats you. A physical or desktop timer, or launching a web one.
- **The chime is a decision point, not a whistle.** It asks one question:
  *stop, or keep going — and which choice do I actually make?* Either answer
  is fine. Unconscious drifting past it is not (that part's just ordinary).
- If your session ran on a promise timer and you stopped at zero, say the
  magic words: **"That was the deal, and I did the deal."** Out loud.
