# Chapter 7 — The Stuck-Task Ladder

This is the panic button. When you're too stuck to read chapters, too stuck
to pick a tool, too stuck to think — **you don't think. You climb.**

## How to use it

Read exactly one rung at a time. Do it, even if it seems beneath you — that
feeling *is* the paralysis talking. If a rung doesn't move anything, go up
ONE rung. Never skip.

Print it: open **`site/index.html`** in any browser and use the *Print the
ladder* button (or your browser's print dialog). One page. Tape it where the
walls live: desk, fridge, door frame.

## The ladder

**Rung 1 — Change your body's inputs.**
Drink some water. Eat a cracker. Feel the sun or a lamp for ten seconds.
Stand up and sit somewhere else, even the floor. Paralysis is sometimes just
  a body complaining. One input change often loosens the knot enough for a
  door to show up. No task action yet. This is a legit rung.

**Rung 2 — Write the task as one sentence.**
Not the project. The *next thing*. "Email the landlord about the pipe," not
"deal with apartment stuff." One sentence, any paper. Reading your own
sentence often halves the fog on the spot.

**Rung 3 — Shrink it in half, then in half again.**
"Email the landlord" → "open email, type the landlord's address in the To
line." Yes, that one. Yes it's tiny. Tiny is the point. You are looking for
the smallest action that is *not zero*.

**Rung 4 — Open the door and stand there.**
Do rung 3's action. Do not continue. You opened the email and typed the
address — that is a completed rep. Cross it off somewhere visible. If you
want to go further, that's [the five minutes](02-five-minute-doorway.md)
now — timer, promise, go. If not, you banked a rep today. True fact.

**Rung 5 — Recruit a body double.**
Text a human the script from [Chapter 3](03-body-doubling.md): no advice,
no help, just be near me for 30–40 minutes while I do my own thing. If no
human is available, stream a study-with-me video and sit with it.

**Rung 6 — Ask the AI shrinker.**
Use Nudge 1 from [Chapter 6](06-ai-nudges.md), then say "even smaller" until
one answer is laughable. Then do that one laughable thing.

**Rung 7 — Tag-team with the two-minute timer plus a witness.**
Body double *and* timer at once: "I'm doing five minutes while you sit here
with me." Set the timer out loud so the witness heard the deal. The promise
is strengthened simply by being witnessed, and the timer fails you only if
you stop early — either way, the rung is climbed.

**Rung 8 — Make the mess visible and pick one corner.**
Dump everything on the task onto paper (all of it — unanswered worries
included). Circle ONE item. Cross everything else out with one ugly line —
not deleted, archived: "not today." Start the circled thing.

**Rung 9 — Lower the stakes on purpose.**
Say out loud: "This doesn't have to be good. This has to be *started*."
Draft the ugly version. Send the rough email. The rough one gets replies;
the perfect one in your head never leaves the building.

**Rung 10 — Veto / renegotiate the task.**
Sometimes the wall isn't yours. Cross-examine it: does this need doing?
Does it need doing *now*? Does it need doing *this way*? Maybe it's real and
the answer is still yes — but *you* need to hear the yes out loud, chosen and
not defaulted. Chosen tasks start easier than sentences handed to you.

## After you climb

Wherever you got to, mark the progress: a tally, a sticky, a mark, a rep.
The [progress card](05-progress-cards.md). Then stop *or* keep going — both
count. The part that was impossible this morning is now "a thing I did
something on." That's the whole game, one rung at a time.
